@topic bio-organisation | Levels of organisation | SS1

E: Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form systems, and systems make up the organism.

Q: A group of similar cells performing the same function is a
A: tissue
W: organ
W: system
W: colony
E: For example, muscle tissue is made of muscle cells working together.

Q: Which of these is an organ?
A: the heart
W: blood
W: a red blood cell
W: the circulatory system
E: An organ is made of several tissues working together. Blood is a tissue; the circulatory system is an organ system.

Q: Volvox is an example of organisms living as a
A: colony
W: tissue
W: single cell
W: organ system
E: Volvox cells live together in a colony but each can survive on its own.

Q: Which organism is unicellular?
A: Amoeba
W: Spirogyra
W: Hydra
W: Earthworm
E: Amoeba is made of only one cell. Spirogyra is filamentous; Hydra and the earthworm are multicellular.

Q: Which is a disadvantage of being a large multicellular organism?

W: cells can specialise
W: the organism can grow larger
W: tissues can share work
E: Large organisms need transport systems because diffusion is too slow over long distances.
